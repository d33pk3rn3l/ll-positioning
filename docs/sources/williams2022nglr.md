# Next-generation laser ranging at Lunar Geophysical Network and commercial lander sites (Williams, Boggs & Currie 2022, Planetary Science Journal 3:136)

Citation key: `williams2022nglr`. Original title: "Next-generation Laser Ranging at Lunar Geophysical Network and Commercial Lander Payload Service Sites". LGN = Lunar Geophysical Network. CLPS = Commercial Lander Payload Service (wording of the title).

## Claim
Single corner cubes of 10 cm (NGLR, full name: TBD - check; and MoonLIGHT, full name: TBD - check) do not spread the laser pulse. Simulations show improved lunar science parameters. The coordinates of a new reflector become better than 1 m after one month of well-distributed data.

## Numbers (with units and conditions)
Format: value | product | frame realization | statistic type. "not stated" = the source does not say.
- New reflector coordinates: better than 1 m after one month of well-distributed data, present-day range uncertainties | simulation | principal axis (PA), centre of mass | statistic not stated.
- Existing reflectors known to 0.2 m | lunar laser ranging (LLR) solutions | PA | statistic not stated.
- Coordinates with uncertainties ~1 m are useful to tie the coordinate frame of the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) and the LLR frame (Wagner et al. 2017) | statement.
- Post-fit root mean square (RMS) residuals of simulated cases: a few mm | simulation | RMS.

## Height reference
Moon-centred coordinates (centre of mass, PA axes).

## Application to a walking quadruped on the Moon (LunarLeaper)
A retroreflector on the LunarLeaper lander could give a lander position at <1 m in PA within a month. But this is longer than the mission of one lunar day. It is a frame tie for the lander, not a navigation aid for the robot.

## Grade
Model (grade C) for the numbers of the new reflector. The 0.2 m value for existing reflectors is from flight LLR (grade A).
