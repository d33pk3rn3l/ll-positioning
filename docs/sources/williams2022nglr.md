# williams2022nglr: Next-generation Laser Ranging at LGN and CLPS Sites (Williams, Boggs & Currie 2022, PSJ 3:136)

## Claim
10 cm single corner cubes (NGLR, MoonLIGHT) do not spread the laser pulse. Simulations show improved lunar science parameters. Coordinates of a new reflector reach sub-metre accuracy after one month of well-distributed data.

## Numbers (with units and conditions)
Format: value | product | frame realization | statistic type. "not stated" = the source does not say.
- New reflector coordinates: better than 1 m after one month of well-distributed data, present-day range uncertainties | simulation | PA, centre of mass | statistic not stated.
- Existing reflectors known to 0.2 m | LLR solutions | PA | statistic not stated.
- Coordinates with uncertainties ~1 m are useful to tie the LRO coordinate frame and the LLR frame (Wagner et al. 2017) | statement.
- Post-fit rms residuals of simulated cases: a few mm | simulation | RMS.

## Height reference
Moon-centred coordinates (centre of mass, PA axes).

## Application to a walking quadruped on the Moon (LunarLeaper)
A retroreflector on the LunarLeaper lander could give a lander position at <1 m in PA within a month, but this is longer than the one-lunar-day mission. It is a frame tie for the lander, not a robot navigation aid.

## Grade
C (model/simulation) for the new-reflector numbers; the 0.2 m existing-reflector value is from flight LLR.
