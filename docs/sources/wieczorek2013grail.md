# wieczorek2013grail: The crust of the Moon as seen by GRAIL (Wieczorek et al., Science 339:671-675, 2013)
Abbreviations: GRAIL = Gravity Recovery and Interior Laboratory (twin-spacecraft gravity mission); PMC = PubMed Central.

## Claim
The bulk density of the lunar highlands crust, from GRAIL gravity and topography, is 2550 kg/m^3. This is lower than the 2800-2900 kg/m^3 used before, and gives an average crustal porosity of 12 % down to at least a few km.

## Numbers (with units and conditions)
Format: value | what it represents | statistic | conditions. "not stated" = the source does not say.
- "The average density of the entire highlands crust is found to be 2550 kg m−3" | bulk density, highlands crust | mean of regional estimates | spherical harmonic degrees 150-310; 360 km diameter analysis circles; regions with more than 5 % mare basalt cover excluded.
- "individual density uncertainties are on average 18 kg m−3" | uncertainty of one regional estimate | average of regional uncertainties (statistic type not stated in main text; Monte Carlo and coefficient uncertainties) | per analysis region.
- "significant lateral variations in crustal density exist with amplitudes of ±250 kg m−3" | lateral variation | amplitude (range) | global map, Fig. 1.
- Porosity: "on average 12% and varies regionally from about 4 to 21%" | upper highlands crust porosity | mean and range | from GRAIL bulk density and remote-sensing grain density.
- Depth: "about 4 km" (average topographic relief) is given as a minimum depth scale of the density estimate.
- Samples: "The average density of the most reliable of these measurements is 2580±170 kg m−3"; sample porosities "from about 2 to 22% with an average of 8.6±5.3%" | feldspathic Apollo rocks and meteorites | mean ± (statistic not stated; reads as spread) | laboratory, cited to their ref. 14.
- Older assumed value: "2800 to 2900 kg m−3 that are used typically for anorthositic crustal materials" | context only.
- Mare: "Gravity and topography over the high density mare basalts are excluded from analysis". "Crustal densities beneath the mare are extrapolated from the surrounding highland values". No density for mare or for Marius Hills is given.

## Depth and region
Highlands only. Represents the upper crust to at least ~4 km (minimum depth scale). Not the mare surface layer at Marius Hills.

## Application to a walking quadruped gravimeter on the Moon near the Marius Hills pit (LunarLeaper)
This is the reference bulk density for highland crust under the mare, not for the mare basalt or regolith that the robot stands on. For a Bouguer correction of metre-scale station height changes on the mare, it represents a lower crustal layer, not the near-surface layer.

## Grade
A (flight data: GRAIL gravity and Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter topography). The density is a model fit to these data (correlation minimisation), not a direct measurement.
