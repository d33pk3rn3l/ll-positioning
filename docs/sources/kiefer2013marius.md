# kiefer2013marius: Gravity constraints on the subsurface structure of the Marius Hills: the magmatic plumbing of the largest lunar volcanic dome complex (Kiefer, Journal of Geophysical Research: Planets 118:733-745, 2013)
Abbreviations: LP165P = Lunar Prospector gravity model to degree 165; LOLA = Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter; Ti = titanium; TiO2 = titanium dioxide.

## Claim
A gravity model of the Marius Hills (5-20 N, 300-315 E) with region-specific densities. The Marius Hills has a large free-air anomaly (236 mGal north, 150 mGal south). Dense basalt must be present at depth, most likely intruded into porous feldspathic highland crust.

## Numbers (with units and conditions)
Format: value | what it represents | statistic | conditions.
- Marius Hills basalt: "Adopting a representative grain density of ~3400 kg m-3 (intermediate between low and high Ti basalts) and a typical mare basalt porosity of 7%, the bulk density of the basalt is taken to be 3150 kg m-3" | bulk density, Marius Hills basalt | adopted model value | based on 5-9 % TiO2 from Lunar Prospector gamma-ray data and Kiefer et al. 2012 lab data.
- "Most measured mare basalts have porosities between 5% and 9% ..., corresponding to bulk densities between 3090 and 3230 kg m-3" | basalt bulk density | range.
- Highland crust under the mare: "a porous, plagioclase-rich rock ... with a density of 2500 kg m-3" | bulk density | adopted model value (Table 1, long-wavelength load).
- "Plausible bulk densities for the feldspathic highlands crust are 2200–2600 kg m-3"; "the most likely feldspathic crust density near 2500 kg m-3" | range and author's preferred value.
- Density contrast of the buried dense body: 440 kg m-3 (preferred, Table 2), tested 315-630 and up to ~950 kg m-3 | model parameter, not a near-surface density.
- Feldspathic porosity cited: "mean porosity of 8.6 ± 5.3%" (Kiefer et al. 2012a, 2012c); far-side upper crust "7.7 ± 2.8%, with an upper bound of about 14%" (Huang and Wieczorek 2012, not opened by me).
- Mare thickness in Oceanus Procellarum: "averaging 400 m thick [DeHon, 1979] and possibly even less".
- Regolith: radar data imply that the Marius Hills are "covered by at most a few meters of regolith [Campbell et al., 2009]" (Campbell not opened by me).
- Regolith thickness values disagree: carrier1991sourcebook gives 10 to 30 m for much of the Moon (called tentative); kiefer2013marius gives at most a few metres at Marius Hills, cited from Campbell 2009, which was not opened (secondary citation). The thickness decides whether a station height change is in regolith (0.38 m) or in basalt (0.9 m).
- Gravity data uncertainty: "30 mGal through harmonic degree 110" (LP165P, pre-GRAIL).

## Depth and region
Marius Hills region. Basalt value is for the mare layer (hundreds of metres, locally km). The 2500 kg/m^3 value is for highland crust below the thin mare. Gravity field is pre-GRAIL (LP165P), resolution ~50 km.

## Application to a walking quadruped gravimeter on the Moon near the Marius Hills pit (LunarLeaper)
This is the only opened source that states a basalt bulk density for Marius Hills itself (3150 kg/m^3, range 3090-3230). It also states that the regolith there is at most a few metres thick, so metre-scale station height changes may be in regolith or in basalt.

## Grade
C (model). The densities are adopted from laboratory data (kiefer2012density) and remote-sensing composition, not measured at the site.
