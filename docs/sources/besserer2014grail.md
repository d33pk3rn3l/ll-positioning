# besserer2014grail: GRAIL gravity constraints on the vertical and lateral density structure of the lunar crust (Besserer et al., Geophysical Research Letters 41:5771-5777, 2014)
Abbreviations: GRAIL = Gravity Recovery and Interior Laboratory; SP-A = South Pole-Aitken basin.

## Claim
A localized admittance analysis of GRAIL data (degrees 250-550, 15 degree radius windows, ~290 km grid). "Mare regions are characterized by a distinct decrease in density with depth". The far side has density that increases with depth.

## Numbers (with units and conditions)
Format: value | what it represents | statistic | conditions.
- "The typical high mare surface density (>2600 kg m−3)" | surface density of mare regions (linear model, ρ = ρsurf + a z) | lower bound, typical | ~290 km resolution; the authors say the parallel-interface assumption "may not be appropriate for mare regions".
- Far-side highlands: ρsurf = 2223 (+44/−54) kg m−3, e-folding depth d = 9.0 (+2.2/−1.8) km, surface porosity 22-26 %, grain density 2917 kg m−3 | far-side only | best fit with uncertainty (type in supplement, not opened).
- Far-side gradient "∼35 kg m−3 km−1"; "typical gradients of 20 − 45 kg m−3 km−1".
- Laboratory reference: feldspathic sample bulk densities "commonly in the range 2400–2700 kg m−3".
- Marius Hills or Oceanus Procellarum: no number in the text. The mare values are in Fig. 1b (map); I did not read a value from the map.
- Secondary reading: deutsch2019procellarum states that Besserer et al. regional admittance gives surface densities of 2750-2900 kg/m^3 for central Oceanus Procellarum. I did not verify this in the map.

## Depth and region
Surface value of a linear density model, near side mare regions, at ~290 km resolution. "Surface" here means the top of a km-scale model, not the regolith.

## Application to a walking quadruped gravimeter on the Moon near the Marius Hills pit (LunarLeaper)
Shows that mare areas have a dense upper layer (>2600 kg/m^3) over less dense crust. It gives only a lower bound and no site value, so it brackets the mare layer but does not give the number at the pit.

## Grade
A (flight data: GRAIL gravity and topography). The density is a model fit at ~290 km resolution.
