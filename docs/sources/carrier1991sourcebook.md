# carrier1991sourcebook: Physical properties of the lunar surface, chapter 9 of the Lunar Sourcebook (Carrier, Olhoeft, Mendell, in Lunar Sourcebook, eds. Heiken, Vaniman, French, Cambridge University Press, 1991, pp. 475-594)

## Claim
Best estimates of the in situ bulk density of lunar soil (regolith), from Apollo drive tubes, drill cores and other data, as recommended by Mitchell et al. (1974). Density increases with depth.

## Numbers (with units and conditions)
Format: value | what it represents | statistic | conditions. Source units are g/cm^3; converted exactly to kg/m^3 (x 1000).
- "the best estimate for the average bulk density of the top 15 cm of lunar soil is 1.50 ± 0.05 g/cm3, and of the top 60 cm, 1.66 ± 0.05 g/cm3 (Mitchell et al., 1974)" -> 1500 ± 50 and 1660 ± 50 kg/m^3 | regolith bulk density | best estimate ± (statistic not stated) | "intercrater areas".
- Recommended table (p. 492): 1.50 ± 0.05 (0-15 cm), 1.58 ± 0.05 (0-30 cm), 1.74 ± 0.05 (30-60 cm), 1.66 ± 0.05 (0-60 cm) g/cm^3 -> 1500, 1580, 1740, 1660 kg/m^3 | same.
- Table 9.5 porosity: 52 ± 2 % (0-15 cm), 49 ± 2 % (0-30 cm), 44 ± 2 % (30-60 cm), 46 ± 2 % (0-60 cm).
- Hyperbolic density-depth model: ρ = 1.92 (z + 12.2)/(z + 18), z in cm; "at z = 0, the surface density is 1.30 g/cm3, and as z increases indefinitely, the maximum density approaches 1.92 g/cm3, which appears to be a reasonable upper limit" -> 1300 to 1920 kg/m^3 | model fit to Apollo 15-17 cores.
- Site spread (drill cores): Apollo 15 "1.62 to 1.93", Apollo 16 "1.47 to 1.75", Apollo 17 "1.74 to 1.99" g/cm^3 | range.
- Specific gravity of soil particles: "we recommend a value of 3.1"; basalt particles ">3.32".
- Regolith thickness over much of the Moon "may be 10 to 30 m" from microwave data, called "a tentative prediction".

## Depth and region
Top ~3 m of regolith at Apollo sites (intercrater areas). Not measured at Marius Hills.

## Application to a walking quadruped gravimeter on the Moon near the Marius Hills pit (LunarLeaper)
This is the density of the loose layer the robot walks on. If station height changes are within the regolith, this density, not crustal density, sets the local slab term. kiefer2013marius cites radar evidence that Marius Hills regolith is at most a few metres thick.

## Grade
B (laboratory and in situ measurements on Apollo core samples, compiled into best estimates).
