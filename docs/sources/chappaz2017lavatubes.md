# chappaz2017lavatubes: Evidence of large empty lava tubes on the Moon using GRAIL gravity (Chappaz, Sood, Melosh, Howell, Blair, Milbury, Zuber, Geophysical Research Letters 44:105-112, 2017)
Abbreviations: GRAIL = Gravity Recovery and Interior Laboratory; FA = free-air anomaly; BA = Bouguer anomaly.

## Claim
GRAIL gradiometry and track cross-correlation find candidate empty lava tubes, including a mass deficit at the Marius Hills skylight (302 E, 14 N) that extends about 60 km west.

## Numbers (with units and conditions)
Format: value | what it represents | statistic | conditions.
- Bouguer anomaly computed "assuming that topographic features have a nominal density (2560 kg/m 3, the Moon's average crustal density)"; "derived from GRAIL data and was determined to be 2560 kg/m 3 [Wieczorek et al., 2013]" | reduction density | adopted value | note: wieczorek2013grail gives 2550 kg/m^3, not 2560.
- "This value is probably not equal to the actual density of the lava; in samples the density ranges between approximately 3090 and 3170 kg/m 3 [Kiefer et al., 2012a, 2012b]" | lava (basalt) sample density | range | secondary citation.
- Schröter extension fit: "density deficit of 2560 kg/m 3, width of 3.75 km, height of 600 m"; fit "rapidly deteriorates for density contrast inferior to approximately 2000 kg/m 3" | model (empty tube contrast) | best fit.
- Marius Hills skylight: "65 m in diameter, 80–85 m deep; has a roof thickness of 20–25 m"; trough "~400 m wide and ~200–300 m deep" | geometry | as given.
- Detection limit: "minimum widths of several kilometers, heights of hundreds of meters".

## Depth and region
Marius Hills and other mare regions. The density is a global highland value used only to remove topography, not a mare value.

## Application to a walking quadruped gravimeter on the Moon near the Marius Hills pit (LunarLeaper)
Shows that GRAIL lava-tube work at Marius Hills used a highland density (2560 kg/m^3) for the reduction, and that the authors themselves say lava is denser (3090-3170 kg/m^3). It gives no new density; it is useful for the site geometry (roof 20-25 m).

## Grade
C (model). Uses flight data, but the density is assumed.
